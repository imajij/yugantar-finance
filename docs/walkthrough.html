01

Who may touch a rupee

Four roles, one direction of travel, and two places where the chain bends on purpose.

When the only payer is the claimant. A fest with one Management account cannot have them pay their own claim, so Finance records it instead and has to say why in a note. The two people stay two people; the log says which pair and why.

Oversight is not authority. An administrator reads every ledger, channel and audit row, and approves nothing. Every read of a conversation they are not in is logged under their name.

02

The five numbers

Every screen showing money shows the same five, and none of them is stored. They are derived in the database from the records themselves, so they cannot drift from what actually happened.

What each person opens on

A member sees one button — claim a receipt. A head sees what is waiting on them and their department’s Available. Finance sees the fest. Management sees a queue of payments to make. Same numbers, cut to what you can act on.

How we know they are right

They are not stored anywhere to be wrong. A fixture test checks the arithmetic against the specification on every commit, so a view that stopped agreeing with it fails the build rather than the audit.

Nothing is a dead end

Where money is stuck because a role is unfilled, the dashboard says so and names the seat. Where a claim needs a correction after review, it goes back rather than being edited underneath the person reviewing it.

03

Money out: claims and contracts

Two paths, one rule. Money is committed when somebody agrees to it and spent when it leaves — and the step that commits is never the step that pays.

Vendors, in one paragraph

A contract approved commits its whole value at once. The advance converts its slice to spent; delivery is confirmed against an invoice; the balance is released and paid, and only then does it close. Paying less than agreed is a reduction at release, with a reason — the difference goes back where the department can see it.

The evidence does not move

Receipts can be added and removed while a claim is yours. From the moment it is submitted the set is fixed — a correction goes back through send-back, so whoever reviewed it knows the record changed.

04

Work, channels, and what catches mistakes

The half of a fest that is not money, and the checks that sit under all of it.

Tickets

One department asking another, on a board: Triage, Backlog, Todo, In Progress, Done. Drag to reorder, keyboard to move faster, sub-tickets and labels to group. A member asks their own head, who takes it further if it needs to go further.

Channels

A channel per department, direct messages between people, and a wall around both. Another department’s head is not told a channel exists. Membership follows the department automatically — joining a team joins its conversation.

Flags advise, never block

Fourteen rules watch for the usual trouble — a duplicate receipt, a round number, an invoice that does not match. A High flag does not stop an approval; it asks for a note, and the note is what the audit log keeps.

Everything is on the record

Every write is one transaction with its audit event inside it, so an action either happened and was logged or did neither. Nothing is deleted; the log cannot be edited, including by an administrator.

Hand it to the college

Transactions, department statements, a payment batch, the vendor and income ledgers, the audit log — each as Excel or CSV, filtered by date, department, state or payee. A department statement prints on one page for a head to sign.

05

An eight-minute demo

Sign in as the Finance HOD, and keep a phone to hand for step two.

  1. 0:00Dashboard. Read the first line aloud, then point at the figure and the bar under it. That is the whole product in one shape.
  2. 1:00Claim a receipt on the phone. Camera, amount, line item. Show the line saying what will be left afterwards.
  3. 2:30Watch Available move. Available has already dropped, and nobody typed it. Do not rush this one.
  4. 3:30Endorse, then approve. Then try to approve your own: the control is not there. Not a disabled button — absent.
  5. 4:30Decline something instead. The dialog names where the reason goes: the audit log, under your name, uneditable. Show it there.
  6. 5:30Raise a ticket, move it on the board. Drag a card — then drag one somewhere it may not go, and let the board refuse out loud.
  7. 6:30Open a channel you are not in. As an administrator. Read the disclosure, then find your own visit already in the audit log.
  8. 7:30Close-out. A department statement, with everything from the last eight minutes in it, on one signable page.
Can the Finance HOD pay money to themselves?

No. Their own claim is approved by Management, and only Management records a payment — never their own.

Can an administrator read my direct messages?

Yes, and the channel header says so before you type. Every such read is logged under their name.

Somebody makes a mistake. Can we delete it?

No. Records are withdrawn, declined or sent back, all visible with a reason. The audit log is append-only for everybody.
